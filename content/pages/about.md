# About Stack Row

Stack Row is a technology publication for people who choose, build, and run business systems. We examine cybersecurity, SaaS, AI, cloud infrastructure, business software, and developer and data tools through the decisions they create.

Our purpose is straightforward: make technology research useful to the person who has to act on it.

## Built Around the Work

Choosing a platform is only part of a technology decision. Someone also has to connect it to existing systems, manage access, understand the bill, and keep the service working.

That operating reality shapes our coverage. We look at the requirements behind a purchase, the assumptions behind a forecast, and the responsibilities that remain after a product is installed.

Our readers include technology leaders, security teams, software buyers, founders, developers, and business operators. You do not need to share a job title to share a question: will this help us do the work better?

## What You Will Find Here

Our articles examine statistics, market findings, and technical concepts. Our blogs apply research and product documentation to questions about costs, access, workflows, and implementation.

The connection between them matters. [Cybersecurity research](/cybersecurity/) can describe an external threat, while a buying decision still needs evidence about your own systems. [AI coverage](/ai/) can explain adoption and investment, while a pilot must show whether a particular workflow improves.

## How We Approach the Evidence

Our editorial standards keep the source and the interpretation visible. They guide what we include, how we explain it, and where we draw the line.

::: cards
### Start With Original Sources {icon:document}
We prioritize official documentation, public agencies, original research, and company disclosures. A source is useful when readers can inspect what it says and understand its relevance.

### Keep the Definitions Attached {icon:tag}
A survey response, a confirmed incident, and a revenue figure describe different things. We retain important details about dates, populations, methods, and scope rather than treating unlike figures as interchangeable.

### Separate Findings From Interpretation {icon:columns}
Reported findings belong to their sources. Our comparisons, questions, and decision frameworks are editorial analysis. Worked examples should be identifiable as examples, not presented as customer results or measured performance.

### Describe the Basis of Product Coverage {icon:search}
Official documentation can establish a published feature or commercial term. It does not prove performance in every customer environment. We do not present document-based research as hands-on testing.
:::

## A Publication With Practical Limits

Technology changes, and published documentation can change with it. An article provides context for a decision; it cannot establish your contract terms, system configuration, or organization’s requirements.

Use the source links to inspect the evidence. Confirm product details with the relevant provider, and test important assumptions in your own environment before acting.

## Corrections and Contributions

If something needs correcting, please send the page URL, the statement concerned, and a supporting source. A specific explanation helps us assess the issue.

We also welcome relevant research leads and contributor proposals. Include the question you want to answer, the evidence available, and any commercial relationship connected to the topic. Sending a proposal does not guarantee publication.

Editorial questions and corrections can be sent to [stackrow@gmail.com](mailto:stackrow@gmail.com).
