# Business Valuation Calculator

A business valuation calculator estimates what a company might be worth using common shortcut methods. This tool offers three: a revenue multiple, an earnings multiple based on SDE or EBITDA, and a discounted cash flow model. Each result is a range built from assumptions you enter. It is an educational estimate, not a professional appraisal.

## How to Use the Business Valuation Calculator

Choose a method, enter the figures and your assumptions, and select Calculate.

- **Revenue Multiple** multiplies annual revenue by a low and high multiple.
- **Earnings Multiple** multiplies seller's discretionary earnings (SDE) or EBITDA by a low and high multiple.
- **Discounted Cash Flow** projects free cash flow, then discounts it to today's value.

Load example values a small owner-run business on SDE. Running more than one method and comparing the ranges gives a better sense of the spread than any single number.

## Valuation Formulas

| Method | Formula |
|---|---|
| Revenue multiple | Annual revenue × multiple |
| Earnings multiple | SDE or EBITDA × multiple |
| Forecast cash flow | Cash flow × (1 + growth) for each year |
| Present value | Cash flow ÷ (1 + discount rate)^year |
| Terminal value | Final-year cash flow × (1 + terminal growth) ÷ (discount rate − terminal growth) |
| DCF value | Sum of present values + present value of terminal value |

Corporate Finance Institute's overview of [valuation methods](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/valuation/valuation-methods/) describes comparable multiples and discounted cash flow, and its [EBITDA multiple](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/valuation/ebitda-multiple/) page explains how earnings multiples are used.

## Choosing a Method

- **SDE multiples** suit owner-operated small businesses. SDE adds the owner's salary and personal expenses back to profit.
- **EBITDA multiples** suit larger companies with management in place.
- **Revenue multiples** suit fast-growing companies that are not yet profitable, including many software businesses. Recurring revenue usually earns higher multiples, because it is more predictable. The economics behind that are covered in [what B2B SaaS is](/saas/what-is-b2b-saas/).
- **Discounted cash flow** suits businesses with predictable cash flow. It is the most detailed method, but small changes in its inputs move the result a lot.

The calculator does not supply industry multiples, because they vary with size, growth, margins, and market conditions. Use multiples from recent sales of comparable businesses, a broker, or an advisor. Growth trends in [SaaS revenue and adoption statistics](/saas/saas-statistics-revenue-adoption/) can help frame the growth rate assumption for software companies.

## Worked Examples

A small owner-run business earns $400,000 in SDE. Using example multiples of 2.5 to 3.5, the estimated value range is $1,000,000 to $1,400,000, with a midpoint of $1,200,000. These multiples are illustrations only.

A discounted cash flow example uses $200,000 of current free cash flow, 10% annual growth for five years, a 15% discount rate, and 3% terminal growth. Cash flow grows to about $322,102 in year five. The forecast years are worth about $876,884 today, and the terminal value adds about $1,374,549, for a total of about $2,251,433. The terminal value makes up about 61% of the result, which shows how much the long-term assumptions matter.

## Methodology and Limitations

- All multiples, growth rates, and discount rates are your assumptions. The calculator does not know your industry or market conditions.
- Results ignore debt, cash, working capital, and deal terms, which change what a buyer actually pays.
- Discounted cash flow assumes steady growth and a constant discount rate.
- This calculator gives an educational estimate. A formal valuation for a sale, tax, legal, or lending purpose needs a qualified professional. The U.S. Small Business Administration's guide to [buying an existing business](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/buy-existing-business-or-franchise) recommends professional help with valuation.
- Currency selection changes the displayed symbol only and does not convert values.
- Inputs stay in your browser. A calculation is saved locally after you select Calculate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do You Calculate the Value of a Business?

Common shortcuts multiply earnings or revenue by a multiple drawn from comparable sales, or discount projected cash flows to today's value. Using several methods gives a range rather than a single figure.

### What Is SDE?

Seller's discretionary earnings is profit before the owner's salary, personal expenses run through the business, interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization. It shows what one owner-operator could take from the business.

### What Multiple Should I Use?

Use multiples from recent sales of similar businesses in your industry and size range. Brokers, advisors, and published deal databases are better sources than general rules of thumb.

### Why Is the DCF Value So Sensitive?

Most of a DCF value often comes from the terminal value, which depends on the gap between the discount rate and long-term growth. Small changes in either rate change the result a lot.

### Is This Calculator a Formal Valuation?

No. It is an educational estimate from your assumptions. Formal valuations for sales, taxes, disputes, or financing should come from a qualified professional.
