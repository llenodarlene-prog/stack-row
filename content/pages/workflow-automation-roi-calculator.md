# Workflow Automation ROI Calculator

This workflow automation ROI calculator estimates what automating a repetitive process returns once exceptions, errors, ramp-up and running costs are counted. It shows hours returned, net value over one to five years, ROI, payback and the volume needed to break even.

## How to Use the Workflow Automation ROI Calculator

Enter monthly volume, manual minutes per item, loaded hourly cost and the share of items that can be automated. Add the exception rate and handling time for items that still need a person, and the current error rate and cost if automation reduces errors.

Then enter software, implementation and maintenance costs, a ramp-up period and the number of years to analyze. Load example fills in an invoice process handling 2,000 items a month.

## Workflow Automation ROI Formulas

| Result | Formula |
|---|---|
| Manual hours per year | Volume × 12 × minutes ÷ 60 |
| Hours returned | Automated items × minutes ÷ 60 − exceptions × exception minutes ÷ 60 |
| Errors avoided | Items × error rate × cost per error × reduction |
| Annual benefit | Hours returned × hourly cost + errors avoided |
| Net value | Benefits over the period − implementation − software and maintenance |
| Break-even volume | Annual running cost ÷ benefit per item ÷ 12 |

## Worked Example

A team processes 2,000 invoices a month at 6 minutes each, or 2,400 hours a year. Automating 80% covers 19,200 invoices. Exceptions hit 10% of those, at 10 minutes each, which uses 320 hours. Hours returned are 1,600, worth $72,000 at $45 an hour.

A 3% error rate at $40 per error, cut by 70%, avoids $20,160 a year. Software costs $12,000 a year, maintenance $3,000 and implementation $15,000. With a three-month ramp-up, three-year net value is about $204,960, and payback takes about 3.8 months.

## Capacity Versus Cash

Hours returned are capacity. They become cash only when the time moves to revenue work, overtime falls or hiring is avoided. Report both numbers so a business case does not count the same hour twice.

## Methodology and Limitations

- Exceptions are charged at their own handling time, on top of the automated share.
- Ramp-up assumes benefits grow evenly over the ramp period in year one.
- Costs and benefits are not discounted. Use short periods for uncertain projects.
- Error savings assume you know the cost to fix one error. Leave them blank if not.
- Inputs stay in your browser. A calculation is saved locally after you select Calculate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do You Calculate Workflow Automation ROI?

Value the hours returned and errors avoided, then subtract software, maintenance and implementation over the analysis period. Divide the net result by total cost for ROI.

### Why Count Exceptions?

Most automated processes still send some items to a person. Ignoring that time overstates savings, sometimes by a large margin on complex processes.

### Is Automation Worth It for Low-Volume Processes?

Often not. The break-even volume shows the monthly volume needed for benefits to cover running costs.

### Do Hours Saved Mean Lower Payroll?

Not automatically. Hours saved are capacity until the time is redeployed or paid hours fall.
