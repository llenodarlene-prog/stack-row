# Burn Rate Calculator

A burn rate calculator shows how fast a company spends its cash and how long that cash will last. This tool finds gross burn, net burn and runway in months from your cash balance, monthly expenses and revenue. Add growth rates to project a changing burn and see the month cash runs out.

## How to Use the Burn Rate Calculator

Enter your cash in the bank and monthly operating expenses, add monthly revenue if you have any, and select Calculate.

Open Growth assumptions to add monthly revenue growth and expense growth. The calculator then projects month by month instead of using a flat rate. Load example fills in a seed-stage startup with $900,000 in the bank.

## Burn Rate Formulas

| Result | Formula |
|---|---|
| Gross burn | Total monthly operating expenses |
| Net burn | Monthly expenses − monthly revenue |
| Runway (flat) | Cash ÷ net burn |
| Runway (with growth) | Months until the projected balance falls below zero |

Y Combinator's [guide to startup metrics](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4i-default-alive-or-default-dead) frames the same question as default alive or default dead: whether revenue growth reaches break-even before cash runs out.

## Worked Example

A startup has $900,000 in cash, spends $85,000 a month and collects $25,000 in revenue. Gross burn is $85,000 and net burn is $60,000. Flat runway is $900,000 ÷ $60,000 = 15 months.

If revenue grows 5% a month and expenses grow 1%, net burn shrinks each month and the cash lasts longer. The projection shows when, or whether, revenue overtakes spending.

## How Much Runway Is Enough?

Fundraising usually takes several months, so many founders start raising with 9 to 12 months of runway left. Below six months, the options narrow to cutting costs or raising on worse terms. Churn eats into revenue growth, so pair this with our [churn rate analysis](/saas/churn-rate-analysis/). For subscription revenue, the [ARR and MRR calculator](/tools/arr-mrr-calculator/) helps set the revenue input.

## Methodology and Limitations

- Burn uses cash in and cash out. Use collected revenue, not booked or invoiced revenue.
- Growth rates compound monthly and apply from the second month.
- Projections stop after 10 years.
- One-time costs, debt payments and new funding are not modeled unless you fold them into the inputs.
- Currency selection changes the displayed symbol only. Inputs stay in your browser.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the Difference Between Gross and Net Burn?

Gross burn is everything a company spends each month. Net burn subtracts the cash it brings in. A startup spending $85,000 with $25,000 of revenue has a $60,000 net burn.

### How Do You Calculate Runway?

Divide cash in the bank by monthly net burn. $900,000 with a $60,000 net burn gives 15 months of runway, assuming spending and revenue stay flat.

### What Is a Good Burn Rate?

There is no single number. A good burn rate leaves enough runway to reach the next milestone and raise again, usually at least 12 to 18 months after a round closes.

### What Does Default Alive Mean?

A startup is default alive if, at current growth and spending, it reaches profitability before cash runs out. If not, it is default dead and needs to raise money or cut costs.
