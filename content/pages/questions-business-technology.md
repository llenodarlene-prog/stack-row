# Business Technology Questions and Answers

Business technology is the software a company uses to run sales, finance, operations, and reporting. These are the most common questions about choosing and managing that software. Each gets a short answer.

## Business Intelligence

Business intelligence turns company data into reports and dashboards. These answers cover what it is and how to choose a tool.

### What Is Business Intelligence?

Business intelligence (BI) turns company data into reports, dashboards, and answers. BI software connects to databases and apps, applies shared definitions, and shows the results visually. It helps teams track performance and spot problems without waiting on custom reports.

### What Does Business Intelligence Software Do?

BI software pulls data from sources like your CRM, accounting system, and data warehouse. It cleans and models that data, then lets people build charts and dashboards. Most tools also schedule reports, set alerts, and control who can see which numbers. Many now add natural-language questions.

[[flow: How data reaches a BI dashboard | Apps and databases hold raw data > Data is cleaned and modeled > Shared metric definitions are applied > Dashboards and reports query the model > Teams review and act]]

### How Is Business Intelligence Different From Business Analytics?

The terms overlap, and many vendors use them interchangeably. Business intelligence usually describes reporting on what happened and what is happening now. Business analytics leans toward explaining why and predicting what comes next, often with statistics or machine learning. Most modern tools cover both.

### What Is a Business Intelligence Dashboard?

A BI dashboard is a single screen of charts and numbers that tracks key metrics. A sales dashboard might show pipeline value, win rate, and revenue against target. Good dashboards focus on a few decisions. They update automatically, so nobody rebuilds a spreadsheet each week.

### What Are Common BI Tools?

Widely used BI tools include Microsoft Power BI, Tableau, Looker, and Qlik Cloud Analytics. ThoughtSpot, Sigma, and Zoho Analytics are also common. Metabase and Apache Superset are popular open-source options. Google Data Studio is a common free choice for marketing reports.

Stack Row compares twelve of these in its guide to [business intelligence tools](/business-technology/business-intelligence-tools/).

### How Should a Small Company Choose BI Software?

Start with where your data lives and who needs reports. List your source systems, then check each tool connects to them. Count report builders and viewers, because pricing often depends on both. Test with real data and a real report before buying. Smaller teams often start with Power BI, Zoho Analytics, or Metabase.

## Technology Stacks

A tech stack is the full set of tools a company runs. These answers cover what belongs in one and how to keep it lean.

### What Is a Technology Stack?

A technology stack is the full set of software and services a company uses to operate. A business stack might include email, a CRM, and accounting software. Project management and BI tools often join them. For software teams, the term also covers the languages, frameworks, and databases behind a product.

### What Belongs in a Business Technology Stack?

Most business stacks cover the same handful of core jobs, and each job should have one primary tool. Two tools doing the same job usually signal waste. The table shows the common jobs and the kind of software that handles each one.

| Job | Typical tool |
|---|---|
| Communication | Email, chat, video calls |
| Customer management | CRM |
| Finance | Accounting or ERP |
| Work tracking | Project management software |
| Reporting | BI or spreadsheets |
| Security | Password manager, MFA, backups |

### How Should a Company Audit Its Technology Stack?

List every tool, its owner, its cost, and how many people use it. Pull the list from expense reports and card statements, not memory. Then flag tools nobody uses, tools that do the same job, and contracts renewing soon. Review the list at least once a year.

### What Causes Software Tool Overlap?

Overlap usually starts when teams buy tools on their own to solve a quick problem. Free trials turn into paid plans, and acquisitions bring their own software. Over time, a company can end up paying for three chat apps or two project tools. Central purchasing and an annual audit prevent most of it.

### How Can Businesses Reduce Redundant Software?

Pick one tool for each job and move teams onto it before the others renew. Cut unused seats at renewal time. Check usage reports in each admin console. Zylo's 2026 data found organizations used only about 54% of provisioned licenses. Unused seats are often the quickest saving.

### What Is Technology Debt?

Technology debt is the future cost of shortcuts taken today. Outdated systems, quick integrations, and tools nobody fully understands all add to it. The debt shows up as slow changes, security gaps, and expensive migrations later. Paying it down means replacing or tidying those systems on purpose.

## ERP and CRM

ERP and CRM are two of the largest systems a company buys. These answers explain what each does and how they differ.

### What Is ERP Software?

ERP, or enterprise resource planning, software runs core business operations in one connected system. It typically covers accounting, purchasing, inventory, manufacturing, and payroll. Because every department uses the same data, numbers match across finance and operations. NetSuite, SAP, and Microsoft Dynamics are common examples.

Stack Row's explainer on [what ERP is](/business-technology/what-is-erp/) covers modules and implementation in more depth.

### What Is CRM Software?

CRM, or customer relationship management, software tracks every interaction with leads and customers. Sales teams use it to manage deals, log calls and emails, and forecast revenue. Support and marketing teams use the same records. HubSpot, Salesforce, Zoho CRM, and Pipedrive are well-known options.

### What Is the Difference Between ERP and CRM?

CRM manages the front office: leads, deals, and customer relationships. ERP manages the back office: accounting, inventory, purchasing, and fulfillment. A CRM helps you win the order, and an ERP helps you deliver and account for it. Many companies connect the two.

| | CRM | ERP |
|---|---|---|
| Main users | Sales, marketing, support | Finance, operations, supply chain |
| Core records | Leads, contacts, deals | Orders, invoices, inventory |
| Main goal | Win and keep customers | Run operations accurately |

### When Does a Small Company Need ERP?

A small company usually needs ERP when spreadsheets and basic accounting software can't keep up. Common signs include inventory errors, slow month-end closes, and finance and operations reporting different numbers. Growth into several locations or currencies often forces the move. Before that point, accounting software plus a CRM is often enough.

### What Data Should Be Migrated Into a CRM?

Move active contacts, companies, open deals, and recent activity history. Clean the data first by removing duplicates and fixing formats. Old closed deals and inactive contacts can often stay in an archive. Map every field before importing, so nothing lands in the wrong place.

## Automation and Software Decisions

These answers cover automating routine work and judging whether new software is worth the cost.

### What Is Workflow Automation?

Workflow automation uses software to run repeatable steps without manual effort. A rule might create a task when a deal closes, or route an invoice for approval. Tools like Zapier, Microsoft Power Automate, and built-in CRM workflows connect apps and move data between them.

[[flow: A simple automated workflow | A form is submitted > A record is created in the CRM > The right owner is assigned > A welcome email is sent > A follow-up task is scheduled]]

### What Processes Should Be Automated First?

Start with tasks that are frequent, rule-based, and error-prone. Data entry between systems, lead routing, invoice approvals, and status notifications are common first picks. Avoid automating a broken process, because automation only makes it fail faster. Measure the time saved before and after.

### How Is Software ROI Calculated?

Software return on investment (ROI) compares the gain from a tool with its total cost. Subtract the total cost from the total gain, divide by the total cost, and multiply by 100. Gains include time saved, revenue added, and costs avoided.

The [ROI calculator](/tools/roi-calculator/) runs the numbers, including annualized ROI over several years.

### What Belongs in Software Total Cost of Ownership?

Total cost of ownership (TCO) includes everything you spend over the life of a tool. That covers licenses, setup, data migration, integration, training, admin time, and support. It also covers the cost of switching away later. TCO is usually well above the subscription price alone.

### How Should a Company Compare Software Vendors?

Write down the problems the software must solve before seeing demos. Score each vendor against the same criteria: features, ease of use, integrations, security, support, and total cost. Ask for references from similar companies. Run a trial with real data and real users before signing.
