# 9 AI Tools for Marketing Teams, Organized by Job

AI tools for marketing cover different jobs, including writing, design, video editing, and content repurposing. Choose a product around a recurring task and the review work that follows.

Jasper and Copy.ai support content and workflow use cases. Canva and Adobe Firefly address visual production. Descript focuses on video and audio editing, while Grammarly helps with written communication.

HubSpot, Semrush, and Surfer connect AI features with more specialized marketing workflows. Their usefulness depends on your existing systems and the subscription required for each feature.

This shortlist uses official documentation checked on October 25, 2026. It compares documented capabilities and practical buying considerations. It does not claim hands-on testing, guaranteed rankings, or independently measured time savings.

## What the Adoption Data Tells Us

The CMO Survey's 2026 highlights report describes growing AI use in marketing. It reports generative AI usage rising from 7.0% in 2024 to [22.4% in 2026](https://cmosurvey-new.fuqua.duke.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/The_CMO_Survey-Highlights_and_Insights_Report-2026.pdf).

That measure concerns reported usage across marketing activities. It is not the percentage of companies buying a particular tool or replacing their marketing teams.

Treat the finding as adoption context, not proof that every AI subscription creates value. Wider [AI investment and production-use statistics](/ai/ai-statistics-investment-production-use/) draw the same line between adoption and a proven workflow. A useful evaluation still needs a defined task, acceptable output, and a cost comparison.

For a small team, one well-chosen product may address the main bottleneck. Several overlapping subscriptions can increase review work and make responsibilities less clear.

The following categories help narrow the shortlist. Product names alone do not establish which features your subscription includes.

| Tool | Main evaluation task | Buying check | Human review still needed |
|---|---|---|---|

| Copy.ai | Repeatable content and go-to-market workflows | Chat access versus workflow credits | Inputs, actions, and generated outputs |

Use this as a task map rather than a universal ranking. A video team and an SEO team should start with different candidates.

## Which Tools Deserve a Place in Your Shortlist?

Begin with the task that consumes repeatable effort. Compare two products against the same source material and acceptance criteria.

### 1. Jasper: Drafting with Brand Context

Jasper positions its platform around marketing content and brand controls. Its product information includes brand voice, style guidance, product knowledge, and marketing workflows.

Pro lists one seat at $59 monthly with annual billing, or $69 with monthly billing. Business uses custom pricing and additional organizational capabilities.

Consider Jasper when several content formats need consistent brand instructions. Test whether approved terminology and product details survive different prompts.

Brand context cannot establish whether a statement is true. Check claims against your source material before publishing. Confirm required collaboration and governance capabilities in the proposed plan.

### 2. Copy.ai: Repeatable Marketing Workflows

Copy.ai describes workflows that combine actions such as text generation and research. Its platform targets go-to-market processes across marketing, sales, and operations.

Copy.ai has changed its packaging recently, so confirm the current Chat and workflow plans on its pricing page. Workflow packages use a different commercial structure and include workflow-credit allowances.

Do not treat the Chat subscription as a price quote for a larger automated process. Define the required actions and expected usage before comparing packages.

Evaluate Copy.ai when a recurring sequence needs consistency. Include approval points and failure handling before connecting the workflow to live customer systems.

### 3. Canva: Visual Production Within a Design Workspace

Canva brings AI-assisted creation and editing into its wider design environment. Its AI product information describes access controls that vary by plan and organization type.

It deserves evaluation when a team already creates campaign assets in Canva. Keeping generation and layout work together may simplify the proposed workflow.

Test the final output at the dimensions you publish. Check cropping, small text, spacing, and brand colors instead of judging only the preview.

Canva permits personal and commercial AI-created work subject to its terms. The user still needs appropriate rights to source material used for editing.

### 4. Adobe Firefly: Generated Images and Video

Adobe Firefly provides image, video, and other generative capabilities. Its plans use generative credits, with allowances and available features varying across subscriptions.

The platform also provides access to different models. Check the model selected for a job rather than assuming every generation follows identical conditions.

Evaluate Firefly when visual generation fits your production process or existing Adobe tools. Estimate credit consumption using the output type and settings you require.

Review [Adobe's current plan and credit definitions](https://www.adobe.com/products/firefly/plans.html) before purchasing. Avoid converting a displayed regional price into an assumed US quote.

### 5. HubSpot Content Hub: Repurposing Existing Material

HubSpot's content-remix documentation describes converting existing material into additional formats. It lists Content Hub Professional and Enterprise as eligible subscriptions.

Some outputs require more access. For example, its documentation specifies Marketing Hub Professional or Enterprise for social-post generation.

That makes subscription mapping part of the evaluation. Existing [HubSpot enterprise capabilities](/business-technology/hubspot-enterprise/) should not be assumed to cover every separate hub or feature.

Start with an approved article and compare the resulting assets against it. Check that the tool preserves qualifications, dates, and product limitations across formats.

### 6. Semrush Content Toolkit: Planning and SEO-Assisted Writing

Semrush's Content Toolkit describes content ideas, SEO briefs, AI-assisted writing, and optimization. Its pricing page lists a Base offering separately from other Semrush toolkits.

The current page advertises $60 monthly with annual billing. Supporting documentation uses different wording around the billing cadence, so confirm checkout terms.

Shortlist it when content planning and optimization need to connect. Check document allowances and whether your required research tools belong to another subscription.

A suggested topic or generated draft still needs primary sources. Search data can support topic selection without proving every sentence the system produces.

### 7. Surfer: Content Optimization Recommendations

Surfer's Content Editor provides recommendations for writing and updating pages. Its wider offering includes content-related analysis and AI features.

Consider it when your team needs a repeatable on-page review process. Check the limits for the editor, audits, and any additional AI capabilities.

A vendor content score is not a search-engine ranking guarantee. Use recommendations to identify possible omissions, then judge whether each addition serves the reader.

Keep verified facts and the assigned search intent ahead of suggested wording. Adding unrelated sections to improve a tool score can weaken the article.

### 8. Grammarly: Editing and Tone Consistency

Grammarly Pro offers sentence rewriting, tone adjustment, and brand-related writing features. Its official pricing lists $144 per member annually, equivalent to $12 monthly.

Monthly billing costs more per member, and Grammarly has shown different monthly rates across its pages. Confirm the current monthly rate at checkout.

Evaluate Grammarly when editing consistency creates repeatable work across writers. Use a sample containing product names and specialist terminology to inspect its suggestions.

An edited sentence can sound clearer while becoming less accurate. Review changes that alter certainty, numbers, limitations, or the relationship between two claims.

### 9. Descript: Transcript-Based Media Editing

Descript lets users edit audio and video by changing the transcript. Its product offering also includes captions and AI-assisted media functions.

Hobbyist lists $16 per person monthly with annual billing, or $24 with monthly billing. The retrieved plan includes ten monthly media hours and 400 AI credits.

Evaluate Descript when interviews, recordings, or presentations need editing and reuse. Check how your planned work consumes media hours and AI credits separately.

Review names, numbers, and cuts against the original recording. A clean transcript does not guarantee that an edited clip preserves the speaker's meaning.

## Compare the Cost of the Workflow

Subscription prices cover different units. A seat, a workflow credit, and a media hour do not represent interchangeable capacity.

The following examples show verified published plans. They are not equivalent product bundles or a recommendation to buy everything.

| Example subscription | Annual base cost | Included purchasing unit |
|---|---:|---|
| Jasper Pro, annual billing | $708 | One seat |
| Grammarly Pro, annual billing | $144 | One member |
| Descript Hobbyist, annual billing | $192 | One person; usage allowances apply |

For illustration, the three subscriptions above total $1,044 annually before taxes or extras. That arithmetic does not show that the combination suits your team.

First identify overlapping functions. A team buying several writing products may still need the same editor to review every output.

Include these costs in the evaluation:

- Subscription commitments and renewal dates.
- Additional members or higher-tier features.
- Credits, media hours, or document allowances.
- Integration and setup work.
- Fact-checking, editing, design correction, and approval time.

Measure cost per approved deliverable rather than cost per generated draft. Unusable outputs still consume staff time and sometimes usage allowances.

## Run a Controlled Comparison Before Committing

Use the same assignment in each candidate tool. A fair evaluation needs equivalent inputs, output requirements, and review standards.

For a content task, prepare a short approved source pack. Include the audience, product details, prohibited claims, tone, and intended format.

Then follow a repeatable process:

1. Produce the same deliverable with each candidate.
2. Record generation time and usage consumption.
3. Check every factual statement against the sources.
4. Record the editing and correction time.
5. Ask the final reviewer to approve or reject the result.
6. Compare the total effort required for an approved asset.

Use several assignments before deciding. One unusually strong or weak output can distort the result.

Keep the evaluation separate from campaign performance claims. Faster production does not establish better conversion, stronger rankings, or higher revenue.

## Set Review and Data-Access Boundaries

Decide which information a tool may receive before connecting it to business systems. Public product descriptions and confidential customer records require different handling.

The same distinction applies to [AI access to customer data](/ai/salesforce-ai-data-access/). A connected assistant's usefulness depends partly on appropriate access and reliable source information.

Review these settings with the account owner:

- Which people can upload documents or connect applications?
- Which sources can the tool retrieve?
- Can the team control data-sharing and retention settings?
- Which actions require approval before publication or execution?
- What happens to access when someone leaves?

For HubSpot specifically, [content-remix access and publishing permissions remain separate requirements](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/blog/repurpose-content-using-ai-with-content-remix). Generating an asset does not automatically establish the right to publish it.

Keep an approval record for material claims and sensitive changes. Review generated visuals for misleading product details, invented interfaces, or inappropriate representations of real people.

## Questions About AI Marketing Tools


### Which AI Marketing Tool Is Best for a Small Team?

Start with the tool that addresses your most repeatable bottleneck. Compare complete review effort and cost using a shared assignment.

### Can AI Tools Replace Factual Research?

No, generated statements still need verification against reliable sources. Use approved evidence and check claims before publishing.

### Do AI Content Tools Guarantee Search Rankings?

No, a content score or generated draft cannot guarantee rankings. Search intent, evidence, competition, and site quality remain relevant.

### Should a Team Buy Annual Plans Immediately?

Test the workflow and confirm the required features first. Annual discounts only help when the subscription remains useful throughout the commitment.

## Buy Around an Approved Outcome

Choose one recurring marketing task and define what an acceptable result looks like. Test two candidates using the same source material.

Buy the option that reduces total effort while preserving factual accuracy and brand quality. Add further AI tools for marketing only when another specific workload justifies them.
