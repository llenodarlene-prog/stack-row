# IaaS in Cloud Computing: Services, Costs, and Responsibilities

IaaS in cloud computing gives a business access to computing infrastructure without purchasing and maintaining the underlying physical equipment. The provider supplies resources such as servers, storage, and networking, while the customer configures the environment and operates the software running within it. For a team moving an application out of its server room, that arrangement changes several responsibilities.

Hardware maintenance moves to the provider, but application security, access decisions, and recovery planning still require accountable owners. Spending also shifts toward service charges whose totals depend on configuration, capacity, and usage.

Understanding those boundaries helps you evaluate a proposal before committing to a deployment. The useful questions concern the workload you need to support, the control you require, and the work your team can sustain.

## Key Takeaways

- **IaaS provides infrastructure building blocks.** Compute, storage, and networking form the core service, rather than a complete business application.
- **Customer responsibilities remain substantial.** Virtual-machine deployments generally leave guest operating systems, applications, identities, and access configurations with the customer.
- **Cloud statistics need their definitions.** Adoption of paid cloud services does not establish adoption of virtual machines or migration of every workload.
- **The server rate is only one cost component.** Storage, connectivity, monitoring, licenses, and operating effort belong in the assessment.
- **Control should serve a requirement.** Choose infrastructure flexibility when the workload needs it and the organization can manage it.

## What You Get With IaaS in Cloud Computing

Under the [NIST definition of cloud computing](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication800-145.pdf), infrastructure services let customers provision processing, storage, networking, and other resources on which they can run software. The distinction from platform and software services concerns what the customer receives and controls.

IaaS in cloud computing is therefore a service model, not a synonym for every cloud product. A virtual server and a ready-made email application can both be cloud services while assigning very different tasks to the customer.

The following components provide a practical starting point for a workload inventory. Their exact capabilities and charges depend on the selected service.

| Component | What It Provides | What the Customer Must Establish |
|---|---|---|
| Compute | Virtual machines or other provisioned processing resources. | Capacity, operating system, software compatibility, and runtime schedule. |
| Storage | Persistent volumes, objects, or shared files. | Access, retention, performance, backup requirements, and expected growth. |
| Networking | Virtual networks, routing, and connectivity controls. | Traffic paths, permitted connections, and dependencies on external systems. |

A provider may offer managed databases, application platforms, and infrastructure services in the same catalog. Assess each product separately rather than assigning the entire catalog one responsibility model. For example, running database software on a virtual machine differs from buying a managed database service.

The first arrangement can leave database maintenance and the guest operating system with your team. The second may transfer more tasks to the provider, subject to its documented scope.

## Cloud Adoption Is Growing, but What Do the Numbers Measure?

In 2025, [52.7% of in-scope EU enterprises used paid cloud services](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20260203-1), up 7.4 percentage points from 2023. That measures purchases ranging from software to computing capacity, rather than the share operating virtual machines.

The survey covers specified economic activities and enterprises with at least 10 employees or self-employed persons. These are European findings, not a measure of American business adoption or every microbusiness.

Among enterprises already buying paid cloud services, [96.44% purchased SaaS, 77.25% purchased IaaS, and 26.08% purchased PaaS](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/SEPDF/cache/37043.pdf) under Eurostat's classifications. The denominator is cloud-buying enterprises, so these percentages cannot be read as shares of all EU enterprises.

| Service Model | Share of EU Enterprises Buying Paid Cloud Services |
|---|---:|
| SaaS | 96.44% |
| IaaS | 77.25% |
| PaaS | 26.08% |

*Source: Eurostat, 2025 EU enterprise survey. Percentages use enterprises purchasing paid cloud services as the denominator. Categories overlap.*

These percentages describe overlapping service purchases within the same population. An enterprise can buy multiple service models, so the figures must not be added together. Eurostat's IaaS grouping includes database hosting, file storage, or computing power for an enterprise's own software.

The 77.25% figure does not mean that this share operated self-managed virtual machines. A survey classification should not determine the responsibility model of a particular product.

Enterprise size also changes the adoption picture: Eurostat reported paid-cloud purchasing of 49.3% for small enterprises, 66.78% for medium enterprises, and 84.67% for large enterprises in 2025. These [cloud computing statistics](/cloud-infrastructure/cloud-computing-statistics/) use employee bands of 10–49, 50–249, and at least 250, respectively. Use those thresholds when comparing your organization, without treating the largest-company percentage as a migration target.

## IaaS vs. PaaS vs. SaaS: How Much Do You Manage?

With an IaaS virtual machine, your team patches the guest operating system and maintains installed applications. A [PaaS deployment transfers operating-system management to the provider](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/security/fundamentals/shared-responsibility), although your application code, data, and access settings still need owners. SaaS supplies the application as well, leaving customers to manage their information, identities, and permitted configurations.

Use this comparison to establish scope before comparing prices. Individual services can have additional conditions or mixed responsibilities.

| Decision Area | IaaS | PaaS | SaaS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main purchase | Infrastructure resources. | A managed environment for deploying applications. | A ready-made application. |
| Guest operating system | Usually customer-managed for virtual machines. | Usually provider-managed. | Provider-managed. |
| Application work | Customer installs and operates software. | Customer develops or deploys applications within platform limits. | Customer configures and uses the supplied application. |
| Retained responsibilities | Data, identities, application security, and permitted connections. | Data, identities, application configuration, and code responsibilities. | Data handling, identities, access, and permitted configuration. |
| Useful starting question | Do we need operating-system or infrastructure control? | Can the platform support our application requirements? | Does the existing application perform the business task? |

IaaS in cloud computing can suit software requiring specific system settings or installation control. If the application fits a managed platform, compare that option before accepting additional administration work. Service models also differ from deployment models.

Public, private, community, and hybrid cloud describe infrastructure arrangements under NIST's definitions. They do not replace the IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS classification.

## Who Owns Security and Maintenance After the Move?

For Amazon EC2, [patching the guest operating system and configuring security groups remain customer duties](https://aws.amazon.com/compliance/shared-responsibility-model/). AWS manages the underlying infrastructure, but your team must still maintain installed applications and decide which connections the instance permits. Assign those tasks before deployment rather than assuming the infrastructure subscription includes them.

### Draw the Line Between Provider and Customer Duties

An ownership matrix turns that boundary into something the team can review. The following is an illustrative governance template, not a substitute for service terms.

| Area | Typical Provider Role | Customer Role to Assign |
|---|---|---|
| Physical facilities and hosts | Operate and protect underlying infrastructure. | Review location, service scope, and contractual requirements. |
| Guest operating system | Supply the infrastructure supporting the virtual machine. | Patch, configure, monitor, and maintain the installed system. |
| Identities and permissions | Provide identity and access mechanisms. | Approve accounts, restrict permissions, and review changes. |
| Application and data | Support the purchased infrastructure services. | Maintain software, classify information, and control access. |
| Recovery | Provide relevant storage and recovery capabilities. | Configure protection and demonstrate that restoration meets requirements. |

### Give Every Retained Responsibility an Owner

Give each customer task a named owner and a review trigger. A staffing change, new connection, or altered workload can invalidate an earlier assessment.

The administrator provisioning a server may not own the information stored there. Keep access approval with the appropriate business or data owner, supported by technical implementation.

A public website and an internal employee application need different access rules. A [Cloudflare web application firewall](/cybersecurity/cloudflare-security/) evaluates application requests on the configured traffic path, while workforce access controls govern who can reach protected internal services. Protecting the website does not establish that employee access is restricted appropriately.

Map both paths before approving coverage, including any alternate route to the application server. Test an allowed request and an unwanted request for each relevant path, then identify who investigates the resulting logs and maintains exceptions.

## What Does IaaS Cost Beyond the Server Rate?

Cost planning for IaaS in cloud computing begins with resource quantities and the rules used to bill them. For eligible operating systems, [EC2 On-Demand compute](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/pricing/on-demand/) is billed by the second with a 60-second minimum; SUSE Linux Enterprise Server instances use full-hour billing. Record the operating system alongside runtime so the estimate follows the applicable rule.

AWS also lists a monthly 100 GB internet data-transfer allowance aggregated across services and regions, excluding China and GovCloud. Apply that allowance at the account's relevant scope rather than deducting 100 GB independently from every server. Public IPv4 addresses and attached storage also require separate checks, so stopping compute should not be treated as eliminating all resource charges.

Record each material component before comparing alternatives. This checklist identifies quantities that need an actual rate or documented allowance.

- **Compute runtime.** Specify machine count, configuration, region, operating system, and running hours.
- **Persistent capacity.** Include attached storage, retained copies, and relevant performance charges.
- **Traffic and connectivity.** Map expected transfers and any separately billed network services.
- **Supporting services.** Account for monitoring, security tooling, support, and required software licenses.
- **Operating effort.** Estimate administration, incident response, maintenance, and migration work separately from provider charges.

This structure also helps reconcile spending after deployment. Comparing an estimate with the invoice becomes easier when both use the same resource scope.

An observability forecast should distinguish the number of monitored hosts from the amount of trace data collected and retained. In a documented [Datadog APM billing example](/cloud-infrastructure/datadog-observability-costs/), five hosts cost $155, indexed spans above the allowance add $42.50, and ingested spans above the allowance add $15. The scenario totals $212.50 under its stated rates and retention conditions, rather than the host charge alone.

Use that distinction when forecasting your own monitoring scope: estimate each meter and apply its allowance separately. Those documented example amounts are not a quote for another account or the cost of every Datadog service.

## From Hourly Rates to a Monthly Budget: A Worked Example

The following example explains the calculation using invented inputs. These are not provider prices, an account quote, or measured customer spending.

### Add Up the Infrastructure Charges

Assume two virtual machines operate continuously during a 30-day month. Each has an illustrative rate of $0.08 per hour, with 200 GB of separately billed storage.

| Component | Hypothetical Calculation | Monthly Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Compute | 2 machines × 720 hours × $0.08. | $115.20 |
| Storage | 200 GB-months × $0.10. | $20.00 |
| Billable outbound transfer | 300 GB × $0.09, after any applicable allowances. | $27.00 |
| Backup | Assumed flat amount for this example. | $15.00 |
| Monitoring | Assumed flat amount for this example. | $12.00 |
| **Infrastructure subtotal** | **Sum of the five components.** | **$189.20** |

Compute represents approximately 60.9% of this hypothetical subtotal. The remaining components contribute $74, or approximately 39.1%, before labor, licenses, support, and taxes.

### Account for Labor and Shorter Running Hours

Now assume operating work requires four hours monthly at an illustrative $60 hourly cost. That adds $240, producing a modeled total of $429.20 before the other excluded items.

Reducing machine runtime to 220 hours each would lower compute to $35.20. That schedule is appropriate only if the workload can stop safely; persistent resources may continue generating charges.

The example shows why infrastructure rates and operating effort need separate treatment. It does not establish that every workload has the same cost proportions or can use the same schedule.

## When Is IaaS the Right Fit for Your Workload?

An infrastructure assessment should connect retained control to a business requirement. Operating a virtual machine makes sense when that control serves the application and the team can maintain it.

Start with the workload's dependencies. Identify system requirements, sensitive information, expected traffic, recovery needs, and connections to services that remain elsewhere.

Then compare the available service models using the same requirements. A platform that supports the application with less maintenance deserves consideration, even if its pricing unit differs.

The following criteria help keep the decision grounded in operating needs.

| Requirement | Reason to Consider IaaS | Condition to Verify |
|---|---|---|
| Specific operating-system configuration | More direct control over the guest environment. | Supported images, licensing, patching, and staff capability. |
| Temporary development environments | Resources can be provisioned for defined work. | Cleanup processes and charges for retained resources. |
| Variable processing demand | Capacity can change with the workload. | Scaling behavior, quotas, and application dependencies. |
| Recovery infrastructure | Remote capacity can support restoration plans. | Tested recovery time, data consistency, and access. |

Avoid treating flexibility as a measured saving. Compare the full operating cost against alternatives, including migration and the remaining administration burden.

Similarly, provider availability is only one part of application resilience. A deployment needs a tested response to relevant failures, including loss of a dependency or administrative access.

## Test the Deployment Before Expanding It

A pilot should demonstrate that the proposed environment supports the work within known limits. Define the evidence required before expanding the deployment.

1. **Specify the workload.** Record users, dependencies, demand patterns, and acceptable interruption or data loss.
2. **Map access.** Identify administrators, application identities, required connections, and restricted actions to test.
3. **Estimate the full scope.** Include resource usage, supporting services, operating work, and explicit exclusions.
4. **Exercise recovery.** Restore representative data and confirm that the application can resume the required work.
5. **Reconcile and decide.** Compare observed usage, test results, and staff effort with the original assumptions.

Retain configuration records alongside the results. A successful test under one set of permissions or capacity settings does not establish performance after those conditions change.

Before production approval, resolve who handles alerts, patches, exceptions, and unexpected spending. Those assignments are part of readiness rather than tasks to discover after launch.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Does IaaS in Cloud Computing Mean?

IaaS supplies computing infrastructure through a service provider. Customers provision resources and generally manage their guest operating systems, applications, data, and access settings within the responsibilities defined for each service.

### Is IaaS the Same as Public Cloud?

IaaS describes the resources supplied and responsibilities retained under a service model. Public cloud describes a deployment arrangement. The terms address different questions about how infrastructure is delivered and managed.

### Is IaaS Always Cheaper Than On-Premises Infrastructure?

No. Compare infrastructure charges, licenses, support, migration, and operating effort against equivalent requirements. Savings depend on workload demand, configuration, commercial terms, and how effectively resources are managed throughout their lifecycle.

### Does the Provider Manage Security?

The provider protects infrastructure within its service boundary. Customers remain responsible for securing their data, identities, installed software, and configurations, with exact duties depending on the services and arrangements selected.

### How Should a Business Choose Between IaaS and PaaS?

Choose IaaS in cloud computing when required infrastructure control justifies ongoing administration. Consider PaaS when its supported environment meets application requirements while reducing the layers your team must operate directly.
