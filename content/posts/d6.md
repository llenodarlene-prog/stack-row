# HubSpot vs Salesforce: Administration, Reporting and Total Cost

In a HubSpot vs Salesforce evaluation, both platforms support sales teams, but plan names cannot establish which fits your business. Compare the same selling process, reporting requirements, permissions, and implementation work before comparing prices.

HubSpot separates access through subscription tiers, seat types, and permissions. Salesforce also combines purchased capabilities with configured access to objects, fields, and records. In either system, the configuration determines what your team can reliably do.

The useful question is which setup supports your work at an acceptable ongoing cost. This research-led comparison provides a buying framework, current pricing context, and a shared evaluation scenario. It does not claim hands-on testing or a universal winner.

## Start with the Same Sales Process

Define the work before asking either vendor to demonstrate its product. A polished demonstration can conceal missing requirements when each vendor chooses its own scenario.

Consider an illustrative company with ten sellers, two managers, and one operations administrator. It sells annual service agreements and needs a consistent handoff from prospecting to account management.

This is a hypothetical evaluation scenario, not a customer case study. Its purpose is to keep the comparison consistent.

Give both vendors the same tasks:

- Create an account and associate its buying contacts.
- Assign a new opportunity to the correct seller.
- Prevent unauthorized access to another team's restricted records.
- Route an exceptional discount for approval.
- Report opportunities by stage, owner, and expected closing period.
- Hand a completed sale to the team managing the customer relationship.

Ask the vendor to identify the subscription needed for every task. Record additional products, paid integrations, and configuration work alongside the demonstration.

Use your own terminology for stages and responsibilities. If the demonstration requires process changes, decide whether they improve the business or merely accommodate software.

## Compare Plans by Required Capabilities

Neither “Professional” nor “Enterprise” means the same thing across vendors. A buying shortlist needs a requirement-to-plan mapping, not a comparison of matching labels.

The following questions establish that mapping:

| Requirement | HubSpot evaluation | Salesforce evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| Seller access | Which users need Sales Seats rather than other seat types? | Which licenses cover each user's actual activities? |
| Business-specific records | Does the requirement need an Enterprise custom object? | Which object configuration and edition support the proposed design? |
| Automation | Which hub, tier, and workflow actions are required? | Which edition, Flow limits, and additional services apply? |
| Reporting | Can the proposed subscription combine the required data sources? | Can the proposed report design reproduce the agreed business definition? |
| Permissions | Do record access and tool permissions match each role? | Do object, field, and record controls produce the intended access? |
| Maintenance | Who owns workflows, imports, and reporting changes? | Who owns configuration, integrations, and release checks? |

These are evaluation questions rather than claims that either product passes every requirement. Require evidence from the exact configuration included in the proposal.

HubSpot's documentation places custom objects within eligible Enterprise subscriptions. That requirement can change the shortlist before user pricing enters the calculation.

For example, distinguish a new field from an independent business entity. A contract renewal date might be a property; a separately managed asset may require another design.

Review whether [HubSpot Enterprise fits the operating model](/business-technology/hubspot-enterprise/) before upgrading for one feature. Define the records, relationships, and ownership the business needs first.

## Seats and Permissions Solve Different Problems

A purchased seat provides access to capabilities; permissions determine how someone can use them. Buying the correct subscription does not complete access configuration.

HubSpot documents Core, Sales, Service, Revenue, and View-Only seats, among other types. Sales Seats provide access to the included Professional or Enterprise sales features.

View-Only access has restrictions, so do not assign it solely to reduce a spreadsheet total. Someone who must edit records or manage tools needs a role and seat supporting those tasks.

Salesforce documents separate controls for object access, field visibility, and individual records. Its [record-access model](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/data_security/data_security_records) includes organization-wide defaults, role hierarchies, and sharing mechanisms.

Test the resulting access with ordinary user accounts. An administrator's successful demonstration does not prove that a seller or manager has appropriate permissions.

Use at least three test identities during evaluation. One represents a seller, another a manager, and the third someone outside the permitted team.

Check both allowed and prohibited actions. The seller should complete assigned work, while the unrelated user should remain unable to access restricted data.

The same preparation supports [controlled data access for Salesforce AI](/ai/salesforce-ai-data-access/). Evaluate what information a user or connected process can reach before adding AI-assisted workflows.

## Reporting Needs a Business Definition

A dashboard is useful only when its calculation matches the decision it supports. Ask both systems to answer the same reporting question using the same underlying records.

For this evaluation, use open pipeline expected to close during the next quarter. Define whether the amount represents total contract value, annual value, or another approved measure.

Then specify the treatment of uncertain records. Decide how to handle missing close dates, duplicate opportunities, multiple currencies, and deals without an owner.

HubSpot's custom report builder can combine supported data sources, subject to subscription and reporting constraints. The evaluation should verify the required relationships rather than assuming any objects can be joined freely.

For Salesforce, ask the demonstrator to explain the report's record selection and visibility. Compare the result as the manager and as an individual seller.

A practical reporting test should retain these details:

- Written metric definition and reporting period.
- Source records included in the test.
- Expected total calculated independently.
- Filters, access settings, and relevant exclusions.
- Explanation of any difference between expected and displayed totals.

Avoid declaring a reporting winner from chart appearance alone. A simpler report with a reproducible total can serve the team better than an unexplained dashboard.

## Current Prices Provide a Starting Point

Public prices establish a reference, not a complete implementation budget. The figures below use US dollars and were checked October 19, 2026.

HubSpot's [Sales Hub pricing](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/sales?currencyCode=USD) shows Professional starting at $90 per seat monthly when paid annually. It separately lists required Professional onboarding at $1,500.

Sales Hub Enterprise starts at $150 per seat monthly, with $3,500 required onboarding. These are starting prices; additional products and account requirements can change the total.

The current [Salesforce sales pricing](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/pricing/) lists Pro Suite at $100 per user monthly, billed annually. It also lists Core at $195, Advanced at $395, and Max at $550 on annual billing.

Do not import older Salesforce edition names into a current quote without checking the mapping. Compare the actual offer, included capabilities, and contract terms supplied for your account.

The two vendors' named plans are not equivalent bundles. A lower listed rate cannot establish that the product satisfies the same requirements.

## A Ten-User Cost Illustration

Use ten paid sales users to illustrate the arithmetic. This excludes the managers and administrator from the earlier scenario until their access requirements are priced.

For HubSpot Sales Hub Professional, ten annual-billing seats produce a $10,800 annual subscription baseline. Adding the listed $1,500 onboarding fee produces $12,300 for those two components in year one.

For Salesforce Pro Suite, ten users at $100 monthly produce a $12,000 annual subscription baseline. That calculation does not establish implementation or other required costs.

| Scoped component | HubSpot Sales Hub Professional | Salesforce Pro Suite |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ten-user annual subscription calculation | 10 × $90 × 12 | 10 × $100 × 12 |
| Subscription subtotal | $10,800 | $12,000 |
| Separately identified HubSpot onboarding | $1,500 | Not included in this calculation |
| Remaining implementation and access costs | Must be estimated | Must be estimated |

This is a baseline exercise, not a conclusion that either plan is cheaper overall. The requirements assessment may eliminate one of these plans before a purchase decision.

For the full scenario, price both managers and the administrator according to their required capabilities. Do not assume those users are free or require identical licenses across vendors.

Also add migration, training, integrations, and ongoing administration. Keep taxes, discounts, and promotional offers separate from the public-price baseline.

## Administration Continues After Launch

The responsible administrator needs a defined operating routine, regardless of the selected CRM. Someone must review access, resolve data problems, and maintain automation as the sales process changes.

Do not accept an unsupported claim that one product needs no administration. Ask each implementation proposal to name the activities transferred to your team after launch.

Separate initial configuration from recurring work. Building a workflow once differs from checking it after a field, ownership rule, or integration changes.

Specify responsibilities for the first operating quarter:

- Review failed integrations and automation exceptions.
- Approve changes to sales stages and required fields.
- Investigate duplicate or incomplete customer records.
- Remove access when responsibilities change.
- Confirm that management reports still reconcile.

Estimate effort using your process and the proposed configuration. A generic administrator-to-user ratio would conceal too many assumptions for this decision.

The relevant [software buying considerations](/business-technology/b2b-tech-statistics/) extend beyond feature availability. Your internal evaluation should identify who will make the purchased capabilities useful.

## Test Integrations and Migration Before Committing

An integration listing is a starting point, not evidence that the required workflow is complete. Verify the objects, fields, direction of synchronization, and handling of changes or deleted records.

Ask what happens when two systems update the same field. Identify the authoritative source and the person responsible for resolving conflicts.

Migration deserves a separate acceptance test. Select representative accounts, contacts, opportunities, activities, and attachments from a controlled sample.

Check relationships as well as record counts. A successful import count does not prove that every contact remains associated with the correct account.

Include a repeatable reconciliation procedure. Confirm key fields, ownership, stage values, and reporting totals before accepting the imported sample.

Request an export demonstration too. Establish what the team can retrieve if it later changes platforms, including relevant relationships and history.

These checks apply to both candidates. Their results provide stronger evidence than broad claims about ease of migration.

## Choose the Configuration Your Team Can Operate

Shortlist HubSpot when its proposed hubs, seats, and data model match the demonstrated workflow. Shortlist Salesforce when its proposed configuration and access model satisfy that same evaluation.

An existing investment can influence the decision without deciding it automatically. Document which integrations, skills, and records you can retain, and what still needs replacement.

Use the evaluation to reach one of three outcomes:

1. Select a configuration that meets requirements at an understood total cost.
2. Revise the scope when neither proposal addresses the required process.
3. Retain the current system when switching lacks a demonstrated operational benefit.

Before signing, require a written list of included products, user access, implementation deliverables, and ongoing responsibilities. Attach the acceptance evidence and unresolved exceptions to that decision.

The better CRM is the one your team can operate and maintain against those requirements. Its logo cannot substitute for that evidence, which is the real test in any HubSpot vs Salesforce decision.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is HubSpot Cheaper Than Salesforce?

That depends on the required plans, users, implementation, and additional services. Compare equivalent capabilities before using public seat prices to estimate total cost.

### Can HubSpot Support Custom Objects?

HubSpot documents custom objects within eligible Enterprise subscriptions. Confirm the applicable limits and subscription before designing around them.

### Does Salesforce Require a Dedicated Administrator?

The responsible person and required time depend on the implementation. Define recurring administrative work before deciding whether it needs a dedicated role.

### Can We Compare Both Products with the Same Trial?

Use the same records, tasks, and acceptance criteria for each evaluation. Confirm that trial capabilities match the edition included in the purchase proposal.
