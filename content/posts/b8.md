# Datadog Pricing: Billing Units and Worked Cost Examples

Datadog pricing depends on the products, usage meters, retention settings, and commercial terms you select. Infrastructure hosts, APM hosts, log ingestion, and indexed events can produce separate charges. A per-host headline price therefore cannot describe every deployment's total.

Build an estimate by separating those charges before adding them together. Identify the billable quantity, apply its allowance, and use the rate matching your purchasing model.

The examples below use public US prices checked October 19, 2026. They are illustrative calculations, not customer invoices or negotiated quotes. Taxes, discounts, and products outside each stated scope are excluded.

## Start with the Billing Unit

Different products measure different activity. A host count does not tell you the volume of logs or spans an application produces.

Datadog's [published annual rates](https://www.datadoghq.com/pricing/list/) include the following selected units. These prices are monthly equivalents under annual billing, not promises of monthly cancellation.

| Product or usage | Annual-billing rate | Unit |
|---|---:|---|
| Infrastructure Pro | $15 | Infrastructure host per month |
| APM with Infrastructure Monitoring attached | $31 | APM host per month |
| Log ingestion | $0.10 | GB ingested |
| Indexed logs, 15-day retention | $1.70 | Million indexed events |
| Indexed logs, 30-day retention | $2.50 | Million indexed events |

Keep the unit alongside every quantity in your estimate. A million indexed log events and a gigabyte of ingested logs are different measurements. There is no universal conversion without knowing the underlying event sizes.

Start the inventory with these fields:

- Product and exact plan or SKU.
- Applicable billing model and rate source.
- Billable quantity and measurement period.
- Included allowance or minimum commitment.
- Retention setting, where relevant.
- Service owner responsible for the demand.

Do not treat this selected rate table as Datadog's complete catalog. Add other products only when they belong in the deployment you are estimating.

## APM Pricing Has an Important Purchasing Distinction

The $31 annual APM rate assumes Infrastructure Monitoring is attached. Datadog also advertises standalone APM at $36 per host monthly, billed annually.

Under the attached model, each APM host also needs Pro or Enterprise Infrastructure Monitoring. The two prices therefore describe different commercial arrangements.

For example, five hosts at the $31 rate produce a $155 APM host charge. That arithmetic does not include the required infrastructure subscription under that model.

Five standalone APM hosts at $36 produce a $180 host charge instead. This comparison concerns host charges only; included usage and additional products require their own review.

The practical question is which monitoring capabilities you need on those hosts. Compare proposals with equivalent coverage before concluding that the smaller APM line means lower overall spending.

This distinction also prevents double counting. If a proposal includes infrastructure, identify that line before adding another estimate for the same coverage.

## Example One: Ten Infrastructure Hosts with APM on Five

This illustrative deployment uses ten Infrastructure Pro hosts throughout the month. Five of those hosts also run APM under the attached infrastructure model.

Assume annual rates and default 15-day indexed-span retention. Monthly APM usage totals 900 GB ingested and 30 million spans indexed.

Datadog's [APM allowances](https://docs.datadoghq.com/account_management/billing/apm_tracing_profiler/) include 150 GB ingested and one million indexed spans per host monthly. For this five-host example, those allowances total 750 GB and five million spans.

The calculation separates the host charges from excess telemetry:

| Component | Calculation | Monthly equivalent |
|---|---|---:|
| Infrastructure Pro | 10 hosts × $15 | $150.00 |
| Attached APM host charge | 5 hosts × $31 | $155.00 |
| Excess ingested spans | (900 − 750) GB × $0.10 | $15.00 |
| Excess indexed spans | (30 − 5) million × $1.70 | $42.50 |
| Scoped subtotal | Sum of the four lines | $362.50 |

This assumes stable billable host counts and no additional minimums or charges outside the stated usage. It also assumes the listed allowances and rates apply to the agreement.

The APM portion totals $212.50. Adding infrastructure produces the broader $362.50 subtotal. Neither amount includes logs, custom-metric overages, or other separately purchased capabilities.

The five-host APM quantities mirror Datadog's documented billing example. The ten-host infrastructure layer is an additional editorial assumption, included to clarify the purchasing relationship.

This is why a useful estimate keeps its scope visible. Someone reading only the subtotal should still understand which products it covers.

## Example Two: Add Logs Without Mixing Events and Gigabytes

Extend the illustrative deployment with 1,000 GB of ingested logs monthly. Assume 100 million indexed events with 15-day retention, priced at the listed annual rate.

These two quantities are independent assumptions. The example does not assert that every 1,000 GB contains 100 million events.

| Component | Calculation | Monthly equivalent |
|---|---|---:|
| Log ingestion | 1,000 GB × $0.10 | $100.00 |
| Indexed logs | 100 million × $1.70 per million | $170.00 |
| Logs subtotal | $100 + $170 | $270.00 |
| Previous infrastructure and APM subtotal | From example one | $362.50 |
| Combined scoped estimate | $270 + $362.50 | $632.50 |

For this illustration, committed usage matches the stated quantities. There is no unused higher minimum, negotiated discount, or on-demand overage.

The combined estimate covers only the listed components. Archive storage, rehydration, forwarding, premium support, and additional products require separate treatment when applicable.

To change the scenario, replace quantities rather than multiplying the whole bill by a growth percentage. More hosts do not necessarily produce proportionally more indexed logs.

## Example Three: Index Fewer Logs

Suppose indexed volume falls from 100 million to 40 million events. Keep ingestion at 1,000 GB and retain the same 15-day indexing rate.

The indexed-log calculation becomes 40 × $1.70, or $68. Ingestion remains $100, making the logs subtotal $168.

Compared with example two, the modeled difference is $102 monthly. The combined scoped estimate falls from $632.50 to $530.50.

That reduction assumes the bill can fall with usage. An unchanged minimum commitment could prevent the same immediate invoice reduction.

It also assumes the reduced indexing still supports the team's investigation requirements. A lower estimate does not demonstrate that removing those events is operationally acceptable.

Changing retention creates another variable. At the listed 30-day rate, 100 million indexed events cost $250 rather than $170. That is an $80 difference before ingestion and other charges.

Evaluate indexing volume and retention separately. Otherwise, two simultaneous changes can obscure which choice produced the savings or lost visibility.

## Host Billing Is Not Always an Average

Datadog documents different [host billing models](https://docs.datadoghq.com/account_management/billing/pricing/). Its high-watermark model uses hourly observations and excludes the highest one percent before selecting the maximum.

Its hybrid monthly/hourly model combines a monthly minimum with hourly charges above that commitment. The applicable agreement determines which calculation belongs in your forecast.

This means an average host count may not reproduce a high-watermark bill. Likewise, multiplying every temporary host by a full monthly rate may misrepresent a hybrid arrangement.

Ask for the actual model before estimating an autoscaling deployment. Preserve hourly observations where needed, rather than relying only on a month-end inventory.

Broader [cloud deployment patterns](/cloud-infrastructure/cloud-computing-statistics/) provide context for infrastructure planning. Your monitoring forecast still needs the specific workload's usage pattern and billing rules.

The examples above deliberately assume steady host counts. They do not simulate autoscaling, short-lived jobs, or changing commitments.

## Custom Metrics Need Their Own Review

Under cardinality-based pricing, metric names alone do not determine custom-metric volume. Distinct tag-value combinations, including host identity, can create separate series.

Adding a tag does not always increase that count. A tag that adds no new distinctions may leave the number of combinations unchanged.

The useful review concerns submitted combinations, not the number of tag keys in a configuration file. Inspect detailed identifiers before adding them to metrics intended for broad operational monitoring.

Datadog also documents Metric Name pricing as a separate model. Do not apply cardinality calculations to an account using different billing terms.

For a budget review, capture the metric's purpose and responsible owner. Ask which breakdowns support a recurring investigation or operational decision. Then evaluate configuration changes against those uses.

Similar care is useful when separating [Snowflake compute costs](/ai/snowflake-ai-compute-costs/) by workload. A shared platform total becomes more useful when the underlying activity has an accountable owner.

## Reduce Costs Without Removing Required Evidence

Datadog's log indexes support exclusion filters and daily indexing quotas. Those controls affect indexing; they do not automatically eliminate ingestion charges for data already received.

A quota can also stop new events from entering an index after its limit. Review the operational consequence before using it as a budget safeguard.

Start with a defined diagnostic requirement:

- Identify which incidents or service problems the data helps investigate.
- Specify the required fields and searchable retention period.
- Separate repetitive low-value events from required evidence.
- Test proposed changes against representative investigation questions.
- Assign someone to review the result after deployment.

Avoid assuming that every excluded event remains recoverable elsewhere. Confirm archive configuration, access, retention, and retrieval requirements before relying on an archive.

Telemetry handling also belongs in the review. Decisions about [cloud security and access](/cybersecurity/cloud-security-statistics/) apply to monitoring data as well as production systems. Cost attribution should not require unnecessary access to sensitive log contents.

## Prepare a Renewal Estimate Finance Can Reproduce

A renewal comparison should use the same products, quantities, and retention assumptions across proposals. Otherwise, a lower total may simply represent reduced coverage.

Use observed usage as the starting point, then explain expected changes. Separate steady requirements from uncertain growth and temporary projects.

Prepare three practical records before reviewing a proposal:

1. A product inventory with each applicable rate and commitment.
2. A usage record showing how billable quantities were calculated.
3. A change forecast identifying the owner and reason for expected growth.

Keep shared costs visible instead of distributing them through undocumented assumptions. If several teams use a platform service, record the allocation method or retain a shared owner.

Also distinguish estimated usage from the final bill. Datadog provides usage and cost views, but reconciliation still needs the agreement and completed billing period.

Begin with the largest charge you cannot explain. Resolve its unit, allowance, quantity, and rate before proposing a configuration change. Repeat until the material lines have both evidence and an owner. That evidence keeps Datadog pricing reviews grounded in your own usage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Much Does Datadog Cost per Host?

The checked annual list price for Infrastructure Pro is $15 per host monthly. Other products and usage charges can increase the total.

### Is APM Included in Infrastructure Pro?

The examples treat APM as a separate charge attached to Infrastructure Monitoring. Datadog also offers standalone APM with different pricing.

### Does Reducing Indexed Logs Reduce Ingestion Costs?

Not if the same volume still reaches Datadog. Indexing and ingestion use separate meters, and commitments can affect realized savings.

### Is $632.50 a Typical Datadog Bill?

No; it is the scoped illustrative estimate calculated above. It does not describe a typical customer or establish your account's price.
