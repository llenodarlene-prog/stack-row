# Editorial QA SOP

**Applies to:** Verdict Point, Fin Loom, Stack Row, Arizona Direct, and Wellness Signal
**Language:** US English
**Scope:** Website blogs, articles, PR blogs, and every platform version.

**Core standard:** Every article must answer the reader’s question, provide verified information, and add useful value beyond existing coverage. Style, formatting, and keyword placement cannot compensate for missing substance.

For Stack Row, the repository rules in `AGENTS.md` (internal-link counts, silo links, kill list, title-case headings) are the specific instructions that override defaults in this SOP.

## 1. Confirm the Assignment

Before drafting or reviewing, identify:

- The primary reader question.
- The intended audience and search intent.
- The article type: statistics, research, explanation, process, comparison, review, or news.
- The essential information needed to answer the question.
- The primary keyword.
- Applicable brand requirements, word count, platform, and link targets.
- The article’s proposed contribution beyond existing coverage.

**QA check:** Does the draft fulfill the assigned subject and angle without drifting into a different topic?

## 2. Answer the Query Directly

- Give the central answer in the opening paragraph or first substantive section, normally within 100 words.
- Begin each substantive section with the answer its heading promises.
- Use roughly 40 to 60 words for substantive direct-answer passages where helpful.
- Allow shorter or longer answers only when they preserve explanation, context, qualifications, and practical value.
- Avoid introductions that only explain why the topic is important or promise an answer later.
- Make key answer passages understandable independently. Name the subject and retain essential conditions.

**QA check:** Can a reader identify the answer quickly and understand it without visiting another page?

## 3. Fulfill the Title’s Promise

| Article type | Required substance |
|---|---|
| Statistics | Verified figures, relevant comparisons or trends, source context, and interpretation. |
| Research | Actual findings, study details, implications, and important limitations. |
| Definition or explanation | A clear definition, scope, mechanisms, examples, and relevant distinctions. |
| How-to | Usable steps, prerequisites, inputs, and important constraints. |
| Comparison | Consistent comparison criteria, supported differences, tradeoffs, and suitability. |
| Review | Verified capabilities, limitations, and suitability, with documented specifications separated from hands-on experience. |
| News or prediction | What happened or is claimed, when, supporting evidence, and what remains unconfirmed. |

**Reject:** A statistics article that mainly explains how to interpret statistics without presenting substantive figures.

## 4. Verify Statistics, Research, and Data

Prioritize relevant evidence that helps answer the question. Avoid decorative numbers or unrelated research.

For each material claim, verify:

- The original source and supporting passage, table, or dataset.
- Publication date and measurement period.
- Population, sample size, and geography where relevant.
- Units, denominator, definitions, and methodology.
- Important limitations.
- Whether the claim represents an observed finding, survey response, estimate, forecast, calculation, or interpretation.

Use primary sources where available. Inspect original studies, official datasets, regulatory publications, company filings, and product documentation.

Do not treat AI answers, search snippets, or competitor summaries as verified evidence.

**QA check:** Does the source support the exact wording and level of certainty used?

## 5. Preserve Accurate Interpretation

- Distinguish percentage changes from percentage-point changes.
- Separate revenue from profit and accounts from individual users.
- Separate access, enrollment, participation, and outcomes.
- Distinguish association from causation.
- Separate market spending from product effectiveness.
- Compare figures only when their definitions, populations, and periods support the comparison.
- Show verified inputs and formulas for calculations.
- Label forecasts, estimates, and hypothetical examples clearly.
- Keep important qualifications beside the relevant claim.

**QA check:** Could a reader draw a stronger conclusion than the evidence supports? If so, revise.

## 6. Provide Additional Reader Value

Review relevant existing coverage before choosing the contribution.

Each article must contain at least one substantial contribution, such as:

- A useful comparison across compatible findings.
- A clearer explanation of how something works.
- A transparent calculation using verified inputs.
- Resolution of conflicting definitions or apparently contradictory findings.
- A practical decision framework grounded in evidence.
- A specific, supported example showing how information applies.

The contribution must help readers understand or do something. Rewording existing articles does not establish added value.

**QA check:** What does this article contribute beyond a summary of its sources? Identify the specific passage or section.

## 7. Never Invent Information

Do not fabricate:

- Statistics, studies, citations, or quotations.
- Interviews or expert commentary.
- Product testing or firsthand experience.
- Local visits or business interactions.
- Case studies, clients, or outcomes.
- Author identities, credentials, or expert review.
- Missing source details or unsupported predictions.

Original value can come from careful explanation and synthesis. It does not require pretending to have original research.

**QA check:** Can every claimed experience, finding, and credential be verified?

## 8. Use Natural, Contextual Internal Linking

Internal linking is required in every article, but using every supplied URL is not required.

- Write the useful explanation first.
- Place links on descriptive phrases already serving that explanation.
- Verify the destination and confirm its specific reader benefit.
- Keep the current article’s central answer complete.
- Default to a maximum of two internal or brand-content links per long-form article, unless specific instructions override this.
- Treat evidence citations separately from this limit.
- For third-party PR articles, apply the same standards to links back to the brand.

Never create a sentence, paragraph, or topic change solely to accommodate a link.

Avoid link wrappers such as:

- “FinLoom examines this in…”
- “Stack Row explores…”
- “Wellness Signal’s article explains…”
- “Read our guide…”
- “For a deep dive…”
- “This breakdown covers…”

**Three-part link test:**

1. Remove the hyperlink. Does the sentence remain useful and natural?
2. Read the anchor. Does it accurately describe the destination?
3. Identify the reader benefit. What specific need does the destination serve?

If no suitable destination exists, flag linking as incomplete. Find a relevant alternative rather than forcing a link or inventing a URL.

## 9. Apply Brand-Specific Checks

| Brand | Additional QA requirements |
|---|---|
| Verdict Point | Verify jurisdiction and effective dates. Distinguish laws, proposals, opinions, and operational advice. Never invent attorney review. |
| Fin Loom | Preserve currency, periods, fees, assumptions, and definitions. Separate historical returns from projections. Avoid guaranteed outcomes. |
| Stack Row | Verify product versions, editions, release dates, methodologies, and test conditions where relevant. Separate vendor claims from independent findings. |
| Arizona Direct | Verify locality, service area, hours, prices, availability, and dates where relevant. Distinguish city, county, and statewide information. |
| Wellness Signal | Identify study design, population, sample, duration, and measured outcomes. Do not present animal or laboratory findings as established human benefits. |

## 10. Retain Keyword Optimization

Apply optimization even when the brief does not explicitly request it.

- Use the primary keyword **once in the main heading**.
- Use it **twice in the body**, including once in the introduction.
- Follow specific brand or brief keyword counts when provided.
- Count headings, body, and metadata separately.
- Keep placement grammatical and natural.
- Never bold the target keyword.

**QA check:** Record actual keyword counts against the applicable requirement.

## 11. Check Language, Structure, and Presentation

- Use US English consistently across all five brands.
- Follow brand voice, sentence-length requirements, word limits, and banned-word rules.
- Remove em dashes, filler, unsupported superlatives, and formulaic promotion.
- Preserve useful context when shortening sentences.
- Use lists for steps or parallel points.
- Use tables for information readers need to compare.
- Add FAQs only when they answer useful questions without unnecessary repetition.
- Keep required heading introductions and list lead-ins.
- Ensure tables, captions, graphics, metadata, and alt text match verified claims.
- Avoid padding, repeated explanations, and forced sales endings.

**QA check:** Does presentation make the information easier to understand without weakening its substance?

## 12. Assign the Final QA Status

| Status | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **PASS** | All applicable checks are complete. Answers, evidence, added value, links, keywords, and writing requirements meet the standard. |
| **REVISE** | Correctable problems remain in substance, linking, optimization, language, or structure. |
| **HOLD** | Essential evidence is missing, contradicted, fabricated, or cannot support the assigned angle. |

A draft cannot receive PASS because its style or keyword counts are correct while substantive failures remain.

During drafting or authorized rewriting, fix failures before delivery. For audit-only requests, report the issues and proposed corrections without silently rewriting.
